# Preface: A Structured Commentary for the DH Era

This project is an attempt to build a structured commentary for classical Japanese texts in the age of digital humanities.

It does not simply digitize a printed commentary. Instead, it separates the text into multiple layers: the original text, word-gloss, literal translation, natural translation, and notes. Each layer has a different function.

The word-gloss layer records the visible sequence of forms, morphology, and functions. The literal translation preserves the temporal order and phrase sequence of the original. The natural translation may integrate the expression for readability, but should not unnecessarily close the interpretive space left by the text. Notes record interpretive decisions, textual problems, and points where traditional commentary may need to be reconsidered.

In this sense, JSON functions as a medium for commentary. It allows the reader to move from the original text to word-level alignment, from word-level alignment to temporal translation, and from there to interpretive notes. It also allows the material to be searched, compared, and computed.

The goal is not to replace interpretation with data. Rather, it is to keep interpretation in its proper layer. Readers should first be able to understand as much as possible from material derived from the original text itself. Notes should support that reading, not overwrite it.
